Why a phrase manual

Your translator app speaks textbook Portuguese. Brazilians don't.

Outside the big hotels, most people you'll meet in Brazil don't speak English: the taxi driver, the waiter, the guy renting beach chairs. A translator app helps, but it's slow in the middle of a conversation, it needs a signal, and it hands you stiff, formal sentences nobody says on the street.

You don't need to learn Portuguese for a trip. You need the right phrase at the right moment, the way a Brazilian would say it.

What the textbook says

Está bem.

Estou dentro.

"I'm in."

What Brazilians say

Tá bom.

"Okay, fine."

Tô dentro.

"I'm in."

Bora!

"Let's go!"

How it's built

Made for a trip, not for a classroom.

Written by a Brazilian

This is the Portuguese spoken in Brazil, not the one from Portugal. Where everyday speech shortens a word (tá, pra, cê), the manual uses the short form.

No grammar, no lessons

Nothing to study. Each theme is a situation. Open it, find the line, say it or just show your screen.

A pronunciation guide

A one-page key shows how Brazilian spelling sounds in English terms: nh like the "ny" in canyon, final -l like "w", te like "chee".

His and hers

When a word changes with who is speaking, both forms are printed: a man says obrigado, a woman says obrigada.

For the bad day too

Lost passport, stolen phone, a trip to the pharmacy. The phrases are there, along with Brazil's emergency numbers.

On your phone, offline

It's a PDF. Download it once before you fly and it works with no signal and no roaming.
